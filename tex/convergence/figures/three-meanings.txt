Three meanings of genomic computation (Epilogue). Three panels side by side.
1. Computation about DNA (DNA is data): a sequence string feeds a layered sequence model that outputs a predicted track along the sequence. Success: held-out prediction accuracy and calibration. Strongest evidence: homology-aware splits, distal-context and mutation interventions. Examples: DNABERT, Enformer, Nucleotide Transformer, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, Evo, AlphaGenome; Hermon DNA.
2. Computation with DNA (DNA is the machine): an input strand binds a toehold on a gate duplex and displaces a reporter strand, giving a fluorescence readout. Success: intended output molecules form at measured yield and speed. Strongest evidence: measured kinetics and yields with leak, sham and replicate controls. Examples: strand-displacement circuits, DNA neural networks, self-assembly, DNA storage.
3. Computation inspired by DNA (DNA is a design hypothesis): a stored catalog of modules, a context-dependent regulator and an expressed plan, tested by ablation against a matched baseline. Success: a measurable advantage that disappears when the borrowed organization is removed. Strongest evidence: matched-budget ablations and transplants with a falsifier stated first. Examples: DOGMA, Evolutor runtime and governance.
Interfaces (double arrows): about-with = sequencing and synthesis; with-inspired = typed molecular backend (a hypothesis, not an implemented component); about-inspired = model directions and design hypotheses (DOGMA and Hermon DNA are tested as models of DNA data).
Strands carry half-arrowheads at their 3-prime ends; the input binds the gate toehold antiparallel. Schematic strands are not drawn to scale and do not depict a specific published circuit.
