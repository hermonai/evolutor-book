\PartQuestion{Can separating stored structure from expressed computation give a useful computational organization, and what would count as evidence that it does?}
The book opens with a deliberately ordinary program. A fixed catalog of modules and a context-dependent plan show what ``stored'', ``expressed'' and ``adapted'' mean as software roles, and why each idea already has a neighbour in computer science. The chapter fixes the vocabulary used everywhere else: DOGMA is the non-Transformer DNA-native direction, Hermon DNA the Transformer-based direction, and Evolutor the theory, compiler, runtime and governance layer above both. It also states the evidence standard: a proposal earns a claim only through a comparison that could have gone against it.
