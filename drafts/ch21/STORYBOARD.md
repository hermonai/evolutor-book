# Chapter 21 storyboard
Author plan, 27 September 2026; independent review remains open.

Question: what must transform when one DNA record is presented on its other strand?
Prerequisites: Chapter 18 strand hypothesis and Chapter 20 complete carried state.
Chapter 22 owns the integrated DOGMA research model and experiment program.

1. Physical duplex and tensor coordinates: antiparallel backbones, same base
   pairing, opposite 5-prime reading order; reverse rows AND complement columns.
2. Dual directional states: shared operator applied to X and RC(X); reverse
   the second output back into the original coordinate frame, then concatenate.
3. Equivariant readout: explicit tied block matrix [U V; V U]; show what swaps
   and what does not. Summing branches loses antisymmetric strand information.
4. Dependency geometry: causal prefix and reverse-pass suffix; their union is
   full context. Exact reversal equivariance plus left causality forces a
   position-local operator under the stated full product-space assumptions.
5. Chunk order: reverse the list of chunks AND each chunk, preserving record
   identities. Transforming each chunk in arrival order is a wrong sequence.

Reference: canonical-base encoder, reverse-complement involution, differentiable
causal recurrence, shared dual lift, tied strand head, exact offline chunking.
Tests cover exhaustive short sequences, gradients, coordinate alignment,
palindromes, empty records, chunk boundaries and deliberately broken sharing.
Twelve worked exercises; five TikZ plates with semantic TXT companions.
